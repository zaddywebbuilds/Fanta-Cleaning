What moves the number

Six things, roughly in the order they matter.

  • How many panes. Not square footage. A wall of french panes is a dozen little windows and prices like it.
  • Height. Ground floor is pole work. Second and third storey is ladder work, which means setting up, moving, and setting up again.
  • Access. Shrubs under the sills, a locked side gate, a deck rail in the way. All of it is time.
  • How long since the last one. Years of film has to be scrubbed off before the squeegee does anything. First cleans cost more than the ones after.
  • Hard water. Priced on its own, because it is a treatment rather than a wash and it does not always come all the way off.
  • Inside as well. Roughly doubles the window count, though insides go faster than outsides.

Repeat work costs less

Nothing complicated behind this. Glass done last spring has a season of dust on it. Glass nobody has touched in six years has a bonded film that needs scrubbing before anything else happens, and that is where the hours go.

Storefronts usually land on weekly or biweekly. Houses tend to be twice a year, outsides in spring and the full lot in autumn. Offices are mostly quarterly.
